Five ranks keep you alive. Eight bust you. That is the whole of the choice, and it does not improve if you stand — standing hands the hand to a dealer showing a ten and asks them not to make one.

Both choices lose more often than they win. The better decision is the one that loses less, and that is the whole definition of better you are going to get.

A four just left the shoe: +1. Aces sit at one end and tens at the other, and both count −1 — the count does not care what a card is worth, only whether it is high or low.

A count of +6 means the shoe still holds its tens and aces. You now hold the best information a blackjack player can legitimately have. It says nothing whatsoever about the next card.

The close

Chosen, not dealt

This last hand is scripted, and it is labelled because the subject of this page is honesty about probability. You hold the best information a player can legitimately have. You are going to lose anyway.

A good decision is not a promise of a good outcome.

Reference
Shoe
The six decks the dealer deals from — 312 cards to start.
Upcard
The dealer's one face-up card. Everything you know about their hand.
Push
Nobody won. Your bet comes back.
Draw
A single next card, on its own.
Play-out
One hand carried from your decision through the dealer's turn to settlement.
Running count
The Hi-Lo tally of cards that have left: low cards raise it, tens and aces lower it. Describes what remains — never what comes next.
