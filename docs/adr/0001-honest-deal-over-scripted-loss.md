# Deal Act 1 honestly rather than scripting the loss

The Explainer's thesis is that a result does not tell you whether the Decision
behind it was good, so scripting the visitor's loss to guarantee the payoff
would make the artefact contradict its own subject. Act 1 therefore deals
honestly and the copy responds to whichever result occurs — including the win,
where the visitor is told that winning does not prove the Decision was good
either. Only the closing beat uses a Scripted Hand, and it is labelled on screen
as chosen rather than dealt.

## Considered Options

The original brief called for intentionally showing a play-through where the
visitor loses. Rejected as written, because an unlabelled scripted draw inside
an explainer about probability is a lie about the thing being explained, and is
visible to anyone reading the source.

## Consequences

The winning branch needs copy of its own, and it turns out to be the stronger
moment: a visitor told their win proves nothing has nowhere to escape to. The
lesson becomes symmetric rather than a trick, at the cost of the page having no
guaranteed emotional beat until the labelled Scripted Hand at the close.
